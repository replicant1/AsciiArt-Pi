ASCII Art Camera — build guides

Live view from the Pi Camera Module 2, rendered as ASCII art. These are the hardware documents that go with the code: what to run it on, how to get it off the breadboard, and what has to cross the wall of the box it ends up in.

Guides

  • Choosing a display A ranked guide to running this on something other than an HDMI monitor — vintage terminals, VFDs, graphic LCDs and OLED modules. Each option carries a live sample of the same scene at that display's real character grid, so the difference between 80×24 and 42×8 is visible rather than asserted.
  • From breadboard to enclosure Taking the Pi, camera, SPI panel and encoder into a self-contained, battery-powered box. A soldered perfboard HAT replaces every friction-fit joint; includes a pin-by-pin cut list for the panel, encoder and shutdown-button harnesses, and a power budget measured on the Pi rather than estimated.
  • Panel connectors and controls Section drawings and specs for the three things that have to cross the enclosure wall — video out, power in and the shutdown button — closing with the sloped console those three decisions imply, drawn rather than described.

Alongside

  • The enclosure, rendered Four raytraced views of the sloped console the connectors guide arrives at — the fascia from the reader's seat, the parting plane cutting both sockets in half, the camera wall, and the lid lifted off the tray. Built from that guide's stated dimensions rather than sketched to look right. Nothing here has been printed yet.

Reading these

Each guide is a single self-contained page: no scripts, no external assets, no tracking. Diagrams are inline SVG and follow your system's light or dark setting. Saving one to disk and opening it later works exactly as well as reading it here. The renders page is the one exception, since it carries four photographs' worth of PNG alongside it — everything else on it behaves the same way.
